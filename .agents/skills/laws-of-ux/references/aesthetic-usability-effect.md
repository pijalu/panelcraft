# Aesthetic-Usability Effect

## Concise explanation

Visually coherent interfaces are often perceived as easier to use, and minor usability
problems may be tolerated more readily. Positive perception does not prove actual usability.

## PanelCraft application

Use consistent tokens, alignment, spacing, and iconography across the canvas, panels, and
dialogs. Pair polished visuals with measurable keyboard, editing, and recovery workflows.

## Common failure modes

- Treating visual polish as evidence that a workflow works.
- Hiding low contrast, weak focus, or awkward interaction behind attractive styling.
- Making light and dark themes visually inconsistent.

## Review questions

- Does the interface remain usable when styling is removed from consideration?
- Have real tasks and accessibility checks validated the positive first impression?
